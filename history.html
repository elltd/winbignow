The term ‘gacha’ comes from the Japanese word gachapon, which refers to a name originally used by the Bandai company for marketing capsuled toys in their vending machines. Today, this extends beyond the physical realm, and has been introduced in online mobile and PC video games, but has also seen a physical resurgence in the form of blind boxes.

This gacha aspect comes in the form of loot boxes, limited-edition characters or cards, and more, all pre-determined by concealed “probability” mechanisms. In a digital context, the player spends a certain amount of in-game money to “jump-start” the gacha, obtaining a random character or item. Depending on the rarity of an item, the player is enticed to play repeatedly in order to obtain what they want. The acquisition of these exclusive items within a limited time results in an emotional payoff that also comes from relationality. Individuals are brought together through both their successes and failures, creating identity through the feeling of ownership.

Sub-communities have formed for those who play these games, which often include those (referred to as ‘whales’) who spend large amounts of personal funds just to obtain the item they want. Gacha games can be considered a form of gambling, however, legal loopholes prevent proper safety restrictions. Similar communities have formed around the blind box sensation, where consumers are enticed to repeatedly buy blind box toys in hopes to attain their preferred version of the toy, or the rarest mystery toy.

When this becomes excessive, addictive spending habits can cause significant harm to individuals ranging from financial, psychological, and emotional problems.
